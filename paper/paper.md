# The Last Mile, Priced in Nines: A Monte Carlo Resilience Model for Converged AI-RAN / Edge-Neocloud Sites

**Margaret Nanyonga**, DIMAGGI AI

## Abstract

When an operator hosts tenant AI at a radio site and calls it carrier-grade, the promise is underwritten by the last mile: access fiber, its alternates, the power chain, and a GNSS-disciplined clock. We model that last mile as a Monte Carlo fault-injection simulation — calibrated fiber cuts, e-band rain fades, 5G-FWA interruptions, LEO flaps calibrated against Starlink's measured ~15 s reconfiguration cadence, grid outages, storms that *correlate* all of the above, and GNSS denial against holdover oscillators — run against a five-rung investment ladder from P0 (single fiber, $1,660/mo in our planning-grade cost model) to P4 (multi-transport bonding + generator + rubidium holdover + local autonomy, $3,780/mo), scored per resilience class: R0 (fronthaul/timing), R1 (control), R2 (tenant AI). Five findings come out of the simulation, the load-bearing ones locked in as test invariants rather than asserted in prose. **(1) A single-fiber site is a ~2.7-nines site** (17–19 h/yr expected downtime by class) — "carrier-grade edge AI" on that footing is marketing. **(2) Bonding works, but only for the tenant**: 5G+LEO bonding is the cheapest rung ($400/mo) and eliminates nearly all of the connectivity share of R1/R2 downtime, yet R0 gains nothing, because a ~100 µs fronthaul budget cannot ride 5G or LEO — the class that justifies the site is the class bonding cannot help. **(3) You cannot bond your way out of a power outage**: every transport rung converges on the same ~99.9% wall (the site's own power chain), worth ~0.3 nines combined, while the generator rung buys ~1.2 — consistent with the FCC's disaster record, in which power failure dominates cell-site outages and to which the storm process was calibrated. **(4) E-band microwave matches a second fiber's availability** at $700/mo less: nominally diverse fiber pays a shared-duct SRLG tax on physical cuts while rain degrades rather than severs an engineered link; the worst $/nine on the board is the second fiber, and a $60/mo rubidium clock closes the GNSS tail that OCXO holdover leaves open. **(5) Availability is not usable capacity**: charging rain fades and lost bond links by capacity share, no transport rung moves R2's usable-capacity retention off ~99.8%, and even P4 reaches only ~99.88% retention against 99.994% availability. The model, every calibration, and every dollar are exposed dataclass fields; the ladder is one `airan-resiliency ladder` away from a reader's own region.

## 1. Introduction

The AI-RAN literature designs the site; this paper prices its promises. The gap it addresses is that availability discussion at the network edge is either anecdotal (an operator's disaster postmortem) or contractual (an SLA percentage with no generative model behind it). We put a generative model behind it: named failure processes with sourced calibrations, a storm process that correlates them, and a policy ladder in which each rung is a purchasable line item. The output unit is the operator's own — nines per class, and dollars per nine — so the model's job is to rank investments, not to predict a particular site's year.

## 2. Model

**Transports.** Each transport carries independent Poisson failure modes (rate/yr, MTTR, capacity factor) with storm multipliers on rate and repair time: access fiber (rare long cuts, storm-tripled repair; frequent short upstream events), e-band microwave (hardware failures plus frequent short rain fades at reduced capacity, storm-tripled arrival), 5G FWA (grid-dependent: it dies when the neighborhood macro's ~4 h battery drains in a wide outage), and LEO (frequent seconds-scale flaps at the constellation's reconfiguration cadence, plus rarer outages). Eligibility is class-based: R0 rides only fiber or e-band; R1/R2 ride anything.

**Power and timing.** Grid outages arrive routinely and during storms (storm-conditional probability 0.7); a site rides a 4 h battery, then an optional generator (96 h fuel, an assumed 6% start-failure). GNSS denial is survived by holdover: ~6 h within ±1.5 µs for OCXO, ~36 h for rubidium, both exposed as calibration fields.

**Classes and scoring.** Per simulated year, downtime is computed by interval algebra (union/intersection/subtraction on event intervals — never summed, so overlapping causes are not double-counted). R0 is down when the site is dark ∪ timing is lost ∪ all eligible transports are down simultaneously — where a degraded transport (a rain fade at 25% capacity) counts as down for R0, which needs the engineered rate. R2 with local autonomy converts isolation hours into degraded operation at a retention factor; R2's retention additionally charges partial-capacity hours (a fade, one lost link of a bond) by capacity share, so retention is a usable-capacity number, not a synonym for availability. Outputs per class: availability, nines, expected time to restore, episode rate, and retention. The headline table uses 20,000 simulated years at a fixed seed; determinism and seed sensitivity are tested, and the seed spread is reported alongside the results.

**Policies.** P0 single fiber → P1 dual fiber (with 15% of physical *cuts* — and only cuts, not upstream equipment events — shared between the "diverse" pair) → P2 fiber + e-band → P3 fiber + FWA + LEO bonding → P4 = fiber + e-band + FWA + LEO + generator + rubidium + local autonomy. Battery (4 h) and clock (OCXO) are held fixed below P4 so each rung isolates one investment. Costs are planning-grade monthly line items, stated as such.

## 3. Results

(Figures: `figures/availability_ladder.png`, `figures/downtime_split.png`, `figures/cost_per_nine.png`.)

At 20,000 years, seed 7: P0 delivers 99.779/99.800/99.800% (R0/R1/R2); the three transport rungs land within noise of one another near ~99.89–99.90% for the tenant classes — the $400 bonding rung matching the $1,500 second fiber — while P3's R0 equals P0's to within noise; P4 delivers 99.993/99.994/99.994%, and the last digit is not significant (across ten seeds at 2,000 years, P4's R2 downtime spans 0.49–0.70 h/yr). Decomposing downtime by cause shows the plateau's reason: after bonding, tenant downtime is essentially pure power, plus an R0 GNSS tail that only the better clock removes; what survives at P4 is roughly one ~21-hour event every ~40 years — the generator that fails to start in the storm that outlasts everything else. The ladder end-to-end: ~1.5 additional tenant nines for $2,120/mo, with the cheapest rung (bonding, $400/mo) the best value among transports and the generator the decisive rung. Retention tells a colder story: R2 usable-capacity retention sits at ~99.8% for every rung below P4 and ~99.88% at P4 — bonding keeps the site reachable, but 0.45 Gbps of 5G+LEO is ~4% of the fiber's capacity. Local autonomy's *measured* availability value at P4 is near zero — with generator and bonding in place, qualifying isolation windows are rare — and the study reports it that way: it is insurance against correlation structure the model does not capture, not a rung the simulation can price.

## 4. The convergence blueprint (design rules the model enforces)

Five rules from Deliverable 1 of the underlying study, restated as constraints the simulation embodies: split the planes and put the IP demark at the site edge (fronthaul never transits the tenant fabric); *classes get promises* — an SLA is written per R-class, not per site; pin the RAN's floor (accelerator partitions reserved for L1 are not schedulable tenant capacity); enforce zero trust at the DPU, not the perimeter; and claim only mature machinery in the control plane — SRv6/EVPN are deployable today, while BGP CT (RFC 9832) and BGP CAR (RFC 9871) are Experimental and sub-50 ms TI-LFA is a design target, not a measured guarantee.

## 5. Related work

FCC disaster reports establish power's dominance in cell-site outages; fiber-cut statistics and DIA SLA floors calibrate the wireline modes; ITU-T G.8271.1/G.8275.1 fix the timing budget; Starlink measurement studies supply the LEO flap cadence; ATSSS (through Rel-17, 3GPP+non-3GPP pairs only) bounds what standardized bonding covers, which is why our bonding rung is an SD-WAN-style overlay, not ATSSS; the reliability-economics companion in this series prices recovery *inside* the cluster, where this paper prices the pipe *to* it.

## 6. What a skeptic should attack

The calibrations are point estimates from dated public sources (fiber-cut vintages especially) applied to one archetype site, and several rates (E-band hardware, FWA outages, GNSS events, the SRLG fraction, generator start-failure) are labeled planning assumptions with no public primary source; the storm process is a single correlation mechanism standing in for many; Poisson arrivals understate clustering beyond storms; MTTRs are means though real repair tails are heavy; the cost model is planning-grade; local autonomy's value is asserted to live exactly in the tails the model does not generate, which is convenient and should be tested against real multi-day disaster traces; and the headline table is one 20,000-year seed — the reported ten-seed spread bounds the Monte Carlo error, and the P3-vs-P0 R0 equality is a structural claim (eligibility, with battery and clock held fixed across those rungs), not a statistical one. Every rate, multiplier, and dollar is an exposed field precisely so a skeptic can re-run the ladder under their own beliefs.

## 7. Conclusion

The last mile has an investment ordering, and it is not the one the pitch implies: the site that hosts tenant AI buys bonding first because it is cheap, the generator because it is decisive, the rubidium clock because the fronthaul class cannot ride the bonded paths it already paid for — and a second fiber last, if at all. A carrier-grade claim should come with a ladder like this attached; the repository makes attaching one a command, not a consulting engagement.
